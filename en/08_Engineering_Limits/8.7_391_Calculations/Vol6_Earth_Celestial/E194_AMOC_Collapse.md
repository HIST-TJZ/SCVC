## §1 Physical Chain: From H-Bond to Seawater Sinking

### 1.1 Water Density Anomaly Originates from H-Bonds

Water's anomalous properties — density maximum at 4°C, nonlinear thermal expansion coefficient, salinity contraction — all originate from statistical mechanics of the H-bond network (0.20 eV/bond):

- **Thermal expansion**: warming → H-bonds break → molecular spacing increases → density decreases
- **Salinity contraction**: ions disrupt the H-bond network → water molecules pack more tightly → density increases
- **4°C anomaly**: competing effects of H-bond network rearrangement

### 1.2 AMOC Sinking Condition

Seawater equation of state (linearized, N. Atlantic):

\[
\rho = \rho_0 \left[1 - \alpha_T(T - T_0) + \beta_S(S - S_0)\right]
\]

| Parameter | Value |
|------|-----|
| ρ₀ | 1027.5 kg/m³ |
| α_T (thermal expansion) | \(1.5\times10^{-4}\)/°C |
| β_S (salinity contraction) | \(7.8\times10^{-4}\)/psu |

### 1.3 Sink vs. No Sink

| Water Mass | T (°C) | S (psu) | ρ (kg/m³) |
|------|--------|---------|-----------|
| Normal surface (winter) | 0 | 34.90 | 1028.04 |
| Freshened surface | 0 | 34.50 | 1027.72 |
| Deep water | 2.5 | 34.95 | 1027.69 |
| **Δρ(normal–deep)** | | | **+0.345 → sinks ✓** |
| **Δρ(freshened–deep)** | | | **+0.025 → does not sink ✗** |

### 1.4 Critical Salinity

Neutral buoyancy condition:

\[
S_{\text{crit}} = S_{\text{deep}} - \frac{\alpha_T \cdot \Delta T}{\beta_S} = 34.95 - \frac{1.5\times10^{-4} \times 2.5}{7.8\times10^{-4}} = 34.47\text{ psu}
\]

Current surface salinity 34.90 psu. Need to dilute by **ΔS = 0.43 psu** to stop sinking.

### 2.2 Timeline

| Scenario | Greenland Melt (Gt/yr) | Reaching Sinking Zone (40%) | Distance to Tipping Point |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|------------|
| Current (2020s) | 270 | 108 | **~183 years** |
| 2050 Low emissions | 400 | 160 | ~123 years |
| 2050 High emissions | 800 | 320 | **~62 years** |

### 2.3 Positive Feedback Acceleration

⚠ AMOC has already slowed 15% (since 1950). Slowing → reduced heat transport to N. Atlantic → reduced evaporation → slower surface salinity recovery → further slowing.

This creates **hysteresis** — even if melt rates are restored to current levels, AMOC may not rebound to its original state. The precise position of the tipping point has model uncertainty, but SCVC's density constraint gives a physical lower bound: at least ~20,000 Gt of freshwater is needed.

## §3 Intervention Approaches

### 3.1 Artificial Salt Addition ❌

| Metric | Value |
|------|-----|
| Annual salt requirement | ~3.8 million tons |
| Annual cost | **~$189B** |
| Energy requirement | Extremely high (evaporating seawater to produce salt) |

> ❌ $189B/yr sustained for decades — economically unsustainable, and the ecological cost of vast evaporation ponds is enormous.

### 3.2 Ice Sheet Reflective Film 🟡

Deploy reflective material over the Greenland ablation zone (~5% of ice sheet area, ~85,000 km²):

| Metric | Value |
|------|-----|
| Area | ~85×10⁹ m² |
| Melt reduction | ~81 Gt/yr (30%) |
| One-time cost | **~$85B** |
| Tipping point extension | From 62 years → 90 years (high emissions) |

> 🟡 A one-time investment of $85B is within the range of international climate finance (compare: global annual military spending $2.2T). But the logistical challenges of polar deployment and maintenance, and ecological impacts, require further assessment.

### 3.3 Comparison with E192 El Niño

| | AMOC | El Niño (E192) |
|----|------|---------------|
| **Intervention window** | **~60–180 years** | **~55 days** |
| Physical threshold | Moderate | Low |
| Most feasible approach | Reflective film + emissions reduction | Stratospheric aerosols |
| SCVC verdict | 🟡 Engineering wall | 🟡 Physics wall |

> AMOC's time window is ~1000× wider than El Niño's. The bottleneck is not physics — it is political will and engineering investment.
