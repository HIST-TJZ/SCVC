Carbon budgets are a more conservative estimate of "locking in" thresholds — once cumulative emissions exceed the budget, that warming is committed:

| Threshold | Remaining Budget (GtCO₂) | Budget Exhaustion Year | Years Remaining |

> Note: Carbon budget years are earlier than trajectory years — because budgets account for "committed warming" (including as-yet-unrealized ocean lag). Trajectories account for "actual warming" (ocean lag defers part of the warming to future realization).

## §4 The Double-Edged Sword of Ocean Lag (τ=50 years)

SCVC derives water's specific heat (4200 J/kg/K) from the water H-bond, endowing the ocean with enormous thermal inertia:

### Benefit: It Gives Us Time

| Year | CO₂ (ppm) | Equilibrium Warming | Actual Warming | "On Credit" |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|--------|
| 2024 | 422 | 1.8°C | 1.25°C | 0.5°C |
| 2050 | ~483 | 2.4°C | 1.6°C | 0.8°C |
| 2100 | ~622 | 3.4°C | 2.4°C | 1.0°C |

> Actual warming always lags equilibrium warming by ~0.5–1.0°C. This 50-year time gap is the **emissions reduction window** — if we drastically cut emissions during this period, part of the "credit" can be avoided.

### Cost: Credit Must Be Repaid

Even if emissions went to zero tomorrow, the 0.5°C of unrealized warming will gradually materialize over the next 50 years. This is like a credit card — you stop spending, but previous bills must still be paid monthly.
