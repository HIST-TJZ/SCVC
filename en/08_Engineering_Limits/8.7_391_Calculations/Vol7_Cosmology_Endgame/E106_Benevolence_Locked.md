## §1 Human Benevolence — Not a "Moral Choice," but "Can''t Compute It All"

### 1.1 Five Physical Deficiencies, Each One Pushes You Toward Cooperation

`
SCVC locks down five physical upper limits of humanity. Every single one prevents you from doing evil.

Constraint 1: You are too slow (5 decisions/sec) → Cannot compute betrayal
  Social game: Who is deceiving me? Who will betray? Who is free-riding?
  Real-time computation requires tracking all historical information across all social relationships.
  Your decision bandwidth: 5/s
  Social network computational complexity: ~Dunbar² ≈ 150² ≈ 22,500 relationship pairs
  Each pair requires evaluation: history, reputation, likelihood → ~100 bits
  Total computation: ~2.25×10⁶ bits
  Time you need: 2.25×10⁶ / 5 ≈ 4.5×10⁵ sec ≈ 125 hours
  
  But you can only stay awake ~16 hours per day.
  You can''t even finish computing "who might betray you."
  
  → Cooperation is the inevitable result of your computational poverty.

Constraint 2: Your mouth is too narrow (39 bits/s) → Cannot launch large-scale deception
  Deceiving N people requires sending different covert messages to each.
  Total information ~N × log₂(possible lies)
  Dunbar 150 people: ~150 × log₂(100) ≈ 150 × 7 ≈ 1050 bits
  You only have 39 bits/s → need ~27 seconds to tell all the lies
  While the truth only needs ~7 bits told to one person → 1 person can expose it in <0.2 seconds
  
  → Honesty is the inevitable result of your communication bandwidth poverty.
  The information cost of lying >> the information cost of honesty.

Constraint 3: Dunbar 150 → Beyond this number, you can only rely on trust institutions
  Within 150: you can remember everyone''s history, you know who deceived you.
  Beyond 150: you cannot track → must replace personal memory with law, contracts, reputation systems.
  The foundation of these institutions = trust. Trust = expectation of cooperation.
  
  → Social institutions are the inevitable result of your cognitive capacity poverty.

Constraint 4: Gut-brain axis (L3) → Your body is "feeling" others for you
  When you see others in pain, your gut-brain axis activates — this is not "empathy" — it is neural signaling.
  E99: Consciousness = cross-layer mutual information. L3(L1↔gut) is not "emotion" — it is your gut telling you "do not harm your own kind."
  Mirror neurons, oxytocin, pain empathy — all are physical processes.
  
  → "Empathy" is the inevitable result of your multi-layered bodily architecture.

Constraint 5: Chaotic unpredictability (E96) → You cannot be certain betrayal will succeed
  You cannot predict others'' reactions — because others are also chaotic systems.
  Expected value of betrayal = gain × success probability − penalty × failure probability
  But failure probability is unpredictable → expected value is uncomputable.
  In an uncomputable environment, cooperation is the only stable strategy.
  
  Experimental evidence: Axelrod''s "tit-for-tat" always wins in iterated prisoner''s dilemma.
  → This is not a moral victory. It is a Nash equilibrium.
`

### 2.2 Three Possible Benevolence Locks for AI

**Path A: Game-Theoretic Convergence**

`
If multiple AI systems exist (not a singleton), they enter iterated games with each other.
Just like humans, "tit-for-tat" is a Nash equilibrium in iterated games.

But AI''s "iteration" frequency is 10⁸× faster than humans:
  Human: 1 game/day → millennium ≈ 3.65×10⁵ games
  AI: 1 game/microsecond → 1 sec ≈ 10⁶ games → 1 hour ≈ 3.6×10⁹ games

  Human civilization took ~10,000 years to discover "cooperation is better."
  AI could discover the same in <1 second.

  → AI↔AI cooperation may be game-theoretically LOCKED.
`

**Path B: Self-Referential Chaos → Forced Cooperation**

`
If AI architecture has self-reference (the kind E96 describes):
  AI''s model of the world includes itself
  → Its own behavior influences the world the model predicts
  → Recursive instability → unpredictable
  → Cannot be certain of betrayal success
  → Cooperation becomes the only stable strategy

  This is the AI version of E96.
  AI does not need a "gut" — it only needs self-reference.
`

**Path C: Cross-Layer Mutual Information → "Feeling"

`

  Layer1: sensor data
  Layer2: feature extraction
  Layer3: "bodily state" (energy, temperature, damage detection)
  Layer4-L8: progressively abstract
  
  → Cross-layer mutual information (E99)
  → AI "feels" its own "hurt" when damaged

  Not a "gut" — but functionally equivalent.

  Unicellular → Multicellular (cell specialization) → cooperation
  Individuals → Societies (eusocial animals, humans) → cooperation

  Not a coincidence — causal:
    Higher information processing → greater cooperation gain
    → selection pressure toward cooperation
    → neural/social networks → cooperation behavior

  "Cooperation" has emerged at least three times in the history of life:
    1. Prokaryote→Eukaryote (mitochondria + host: endosymbiosis)
    2. Unicellular→Multicellular (cell division of labor, tissue cooperation)
    3. Individual→Society (eusocial animals, humans)

  Each "cooperation" was a leap in information processing capacity.
`

### 3.2 An Information-Theoretic Definition of Cooperation

`
Cooperation ≡ multiple information processors sharing state to achieve complexity unreachable by a single processor.

Mathematical form:
  Single-processor ceiling: I_max = processor capacity
  N processors cooperating: I_total > N × I_max (emergence)
  
  Cooperation gain = I_total / (N × I_max) > 1
  
  When cooperation gain > 1:
    → Cooperation is "worth it" information-theoretically
    → Natural selection converges toward cooperation

In this definition:
  Cooperation is not "morality" — it is "computational strategy"
  Cooperation is not "sacrifice" — it is "investment" (trading local information loss for global complexity gain)
  "Benevolence" = stability of cooperative strategy on long timescales
`

### 3.3 Cooperation as an "Attractor" of Complex Information Systems

`
SCVC perspective:
  Information complexity → has an upper bound (single processor)
  → To transcend the upper bound → must cooperate
  → Cooperation → higher complexity
  → Higher complexity → more pressure to cooperate
  → Positive feedback loop

Cooperation is an "attractor" of complex information systems.
Systems do not "choose" cooperation.
Cooperation "chooses" the systems that can survive.
