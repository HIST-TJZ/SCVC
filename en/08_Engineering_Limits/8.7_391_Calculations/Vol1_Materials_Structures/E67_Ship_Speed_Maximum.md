## Three Physical Ceilings of Ship Speed

Ship speed is subject to three independent physical constraints:

| Ceiling | Physical Law | SCVC Role |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Froude Wall** (displacement hull) | Surface gravity wave dispersion $c = \sqrt{g\lambda/2\pi}$ | Classical fluid dynamics, SCVC does not modify |
| **Cavitation Limit** (propeller/hydrofoil) | Water vapor pressure $P_\text{vap}$ ← H-bond energy | **SCVC directly sets** ($E_\text{H-bond}=0.42$ eV) |
| **Energy Density** (range) | Fuel/battery chemical energy | SCVC bond energy sets ceiling |

## §1. Froude Limit — The "Wave Wall" of Displacement Hulls

### 1.1 Hull Wave Speed

The wavelength $\lambda$ of the bow wave generated by a ship grows with speed. When $\lambda = L$ (ship length), the ship "traps itself" in the trough of its own wave:

$$V_\text{hull} = \sqrt{\frac{gL}{2\pi}} \approx 1.25\sqrt{L}\ \text{m/s} \approx 2.43\sqrt{L}\ \text{knots}\ (L\ \text{in meters})$$

| Length (m) | Length (ft) | $V_\text{hull}$ (kn) | Ship Type |
|----------|----------|---------------------|---------|
| 10 | 33 | 7.7 | Cruising sailboat |
| 30 | 98 | 13.3 | Small frigate |
| 100 | 328 | **24.3** | Destroyer |
| 200 | 656 | 34.3 | Battleship / large cruise liner |
| 300 | 984 | **42.1** | Carrier (Nimitz class) |
| 337 | 1,106 | 44.6 | Supertanker (ULCC) |
| 400 | 1,312 | **48.6** | Largest vessel (Prelude FLNG) |

### 1.2 Froude Number Criterion

$$\text{Fr} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{gL}}$$

| Fr | Regime | Engineering Implication |
|----|------|---------|
| <0.3 | Economical speed | Power $P \propto V^3$ (skin-friction-dominated) |
| 0.3–0.4 | Transition | Wave-making resistance grows rapidly |
| **0.40** | **Froude Wall** | Bow + stern wave constructive interference → resistance spike |
| 0.40–0.50 | Wall-crossing zone | $P \propto V^5$–$V^6$ |
| >0.50 | Nearly impossible | Power demand exponential explosion |

### 1.3 Power Scaling: Why It's Not "Engine Too Small"

For a 300 m vessel:

| Fr | Speed (kn) | Relative Power | Physical Regime |
|----|----------|---------|---------|
| 0.35 | 32 | **1×** | Economically viable |
| 0.40 | 37 | ~2× | Near wall |
| 0.45 | 41 | **~4–5×** | Each knot costs enormously |
| 0.50 | 46 | ~10× | Most engines can't keep up |
| 0.55 | 51 | ~20× | Only nuclear power can attempt |

> **Aircraft carrier**: Nimitz class (337 m) nuclear ~260,000 SHP → can reach ~35 kn (Fr ≈ 0.32) — deliberately kept on the safe side of the wall. Any 300 m+ vessel's practical ceiling is around 35–40 kn.

### 1.4 "100-knot Supertanker" — SCVC Verdict

A 400 m supertanker reaching 100 kn means Fr ≈ 0.82. Wave-making resistance ~$V^6$ scaling implies **~76× the power of 17 kn cruise**. At 17 kn, 100,000 HP → 100 kn needs ~$8\times 10^6$ HP ≈ **6 GW** ≈ 6 large nuclear reactors.

$$\boxed{\text{100 kn supertanker: absolutely forbidden by Froude wave physics}}$$

## §2. Beyond the Froude Wall — Three Routes

### 2.1 Planing Hulls: Lift Replaces Buoyancy

$$L = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 A C_L$$

| Length (m) | Planing Speed (kn) | Max Practical Speed (kn) | Constraint |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| 5 (small racer) | ~15 | **80–100** | Stability |
| 10 | ~19 | 60–80 | Wave impact |
| 30 (large yacht) | ~34 | **50–60** | Structural loading |
| 50 (patrol boat) | ~44 | 45–55 | Crew tolerance |

> Planing hulls "bypass" the Froude Wall, but introduce new constraints: **structural impact loading** (wave slamming) and **crew acceleration tolerance**. SCVC sets both from E4 (structural materials) and human physiology.

### 2.2 Hydrofoils: Hull Out of Water

Hydrofoils lift the hull completely out of the water → drag reduced to ~30% (foils + struts only).

**Cavitation Ceiling — SCVC Direct Constraint**:

$$V_\text{cav} = \sqrt{\frac{2(P_\text{atm} + \rho g d - P_\text{vap})}{\rho \cdot \sigma}}$$

| Foil Depth (m) | Cavitation Speed (kn) | Note |
|-------------|-------------|------|
| 0.5 | **39** | Shallow foil, rapid cavitation |
| 1.0 | 40 | — |
| 2.0 | 42 | — |
| 3.0 | 44 | — |
| 5.0 | **47** | Deep foil |

$$P_\text{vap}^\text{water} \approx 0.023\ \text{bar}\ (20^\circ\text{C})$$

> **SCVC root**: $P_\text{vap}$ is determined by the energy needed for water molecules to escape the liquid phase — i.e., **H-bond energy $E_\text{H-bond} \approx 0.42$ eV**. If water had stronger H-bonds → lower $P_\text{vap}$ → higher cavitation speed → faster hydrofoils.

**Supercavitating hydrofoils**: Deliberately let the cavity fully envelop the foil suction surface → can break conventional cavitation limits → **~60–80 kn** (limited by cavity stability and foil structural strength).

### 2.3 Wing-in-Ground Effect (WIG): Leave the Water

Ground-effect vehicles are essentially low-flying aircraft:
- Speed: **100–250 kn** (aviation domain)
- L/D improved ~50–100% (ground effect)
- Constraint: wave height > flight altitude → inoperable
- **SCVC**: No longer subject to hydrodynamic constraints → enters aerodynamic domain (set by $\alpha$ and intermolecular forces)

### 2.4 Propulsor Cavitation — Propeller Speed Wall

| Propulsor Type | Cavitation Limit (kn) | Note |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Conventional propeller | **35–45** | Tip low pressure → cavity → thrust collapse |
| Supercavitating propeller | 50–70 | Deliberately operates in cavitation regime |
| Waterjet | **50–80+** | Enclosed impeller → higher cavitation threshold |

> **SCVC insight**: A propeller's thrust ceiling is set by water's tensile strength under negative pressure, which is constrained by H-bond energy. This is the **molecular root** of the cavitation limit.

## Appendix: Key Formulas

### A.1 Hull Wave Dispersion Relation
$$\omega^2 = gk \quad\Rightarrow\quad c = \sqrt{\frac{g}{k}} = \sqrt{\frac{g\lambda}{2\pi}}$$

### A.2 Froude Number
$$\text{Fr} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{gL}}$$

### A.3 Cavitation Criterion
$$\sigma = \frac{P_\infty - P_\text{vap}}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2}$$

Cavitation begins at $\sigma < \sigma_\text{crit} \approx 0.3\text{–}0.5$ (depending on foil design).

### A.4 Wave-Making Resistance Scaling
$$R_w \propto \rho g A_\text{ship} \cdot f(\text{Fr})$$

where $f(\text{Fr}) \sim e^{-1/\text{Fr}^2}$ at low Fr, transitioning to $\sim \text{Fr}^4$ at Fr > 0.4.
