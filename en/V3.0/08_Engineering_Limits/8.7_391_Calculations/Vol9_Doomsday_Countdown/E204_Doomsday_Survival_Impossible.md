## §1. The Physical Ceiling of Individual Survival: 13.8 Days

### 1.1 How Much Can One Person Carry?

`
Single-person backpack limit (50 kg, fit adult):

  Optimal allocation: water 41.4 kg + food 8.6 kg
  Water consumption: 3 L/day
  Food consumption: ~0.63 kg/day (2,500 kcal ÷ 4,000 kcal/kg)
  
  Maximum self-sustaining time: 50 / (3 + 0.625) ≈ 13.8 days
  
  Daily travel distance: ~35 km → total activity radius: ~480 km
`

| Allocation | Water (kg) | Food (kg) | Self-sustaining (days) | Outcome |
|-----------|-----------|----------|----------------------|---------|
| Water only | 50 | 0 | 16.7 | Starve before water runs out |
| Food only | 0 | 50 | 80.0 | Die of thirst before food runs out |
| 25 water + 25 food | 25 | 25 | 8.3 | One runs out first |
| **Optimal** | **41.4** | **8.6** | **13.8** | **Both exhausted simultaneously** |

`
SCVC: Human metabolic rate ~100W (basal metabolism)
  → This is chemical energy released by C-H/C-O bond breaking
  → There is no "superhuman who needs no food or water" — this is biochemistry, not willpower

Water source constraint:
  Surface accessible water source spacing: 1-50 km
  Daily travel ceiling: ~35 km
  → If water source spacing > 35 km → cannot reach before exhaustion → death
  → Drought / nuclear contamination / CBRN → usable water sources → zero → death within 3 days
  
Conclusion: Without resupply, a single person's maximum survival radius is 480 km,
      maximum survival time is 13.8 days. This is the physical ceiling.
      The premise of "hide in the mountains and live by hunting" requires: mountains have game + water sources +
      game continues to appear + you won't get injured + no medical needs.
      → None of these premises hold after civilizational collapse.
`

## §4. Bunker Viability — Why Bunkers Fail

### 4.1 The Bunker Paradox

`
A bunker is a CLOSED system.
Closed systems have finite lifespans.

Bunker lifespan components:
  → Food reserves: 2-20 years (depending on investment)
  → Water filtration: 5-15 years (membrane/filter lifespan)
  → Air filtration (CBRN): 2-10 years (activated carbon saturation)
  → Power (diesel generator): 2-5 years (fuel storage, mechanical wear)
  → Power (solar + battery): 10-20 years (panel degradation, battery cycling)
  → Spare parts: 5-15 years (finite inventory)
  → Medicine: 2-5 years (expiration dates)
  
  Maximum realistic bunker lifespan: ~20 years (component-level)
  
CO₂ atmospheric lifetime: 100-1,000 years
Nuclear winter duration: 5-20 years (depending on scenario)
Climate disruption: centuries
  
  → τ_bunker << τ_threat
  → You die inside the bunker waiting for a world that will never be habitable again
  → Or you emerge into a world that is still lethal
  → "The bunker is a tomb with a delayed burial."
`

## §5. Knowledge Decay — Generational Amnesia

### 5.1 How Fast Knowledge Dies

`
Without continuous institutional transmission:

Knowledge retention per generation: K_n = K_0 × (1 − δ)^n
  δ ≈ 0.35 (35% loss per generation without formal education systems)
  n = generation number (25 years/generation)

After 1 generation (25 years):
  → K_1 = 65% of original knowledge
  → Still have: basic agriculture, simple metalwork, reading, arithmetic
  → Lost: calculus, advanced chemistry, semiconductor physics, molecular biology

After 2 generations (50 years):
  → K_2 = 42% of original knowledge
  → Still have: subsistence farming, blacksmithing, basic literacy
  → Lost: electricity generation, antibiotics, internal combustion engines

After 3 generations (75 years):
  → K_3 = 27% of original knowledge
  → Still have: oral tradition of "there used to be machines that flew"
  → Lost: everything that made modern civilization possible

  → Your grandchildren: Stone Age hunters
  → They do not know what "electricity" is
  → They do not know that there was once a civilization that could fly to the moon
  → And they cannot recover that knowledge — because the BOOKS decay too
`

### 5.2 Why "We'll Just Read the Books" Doesn't Work

`
→ Paper decays (50-500 years depending on conditions)
→ Digital storage decays (hard drives ~5-10 years, SSDs ~10-50 years)
→ Languages drift (English from 500 years ago is barely readable)
→ Without teachers, textbooks are incomprehensible
  → Try learning quantum mechanics from a textbook with no teacher, no internet, no lab
  → Now try doing it while also farming your own food and defending against raiders

→ Knowledge is not stored in books. It is stored in PEOPLE.
→ People who can read the books, understand the context, apply the knowledge.
→ When those people die without successors → the knowledge dies with them.
→ "Books in a bunker" = "gravestones with writing. No one left who can read them."
`

## §6. Genetic Bottleneck — Inbreeding Extinction

`
Population genetics — minimum viable population:

  → 50 people: inbreeding depression within 2-3 generations
    → Recessive genetic diseases emerge (everyone is cousins)
    → Fertility declines → population shrinks → extinction in 5-8 generations
    
  → 500 people: avoids the worst inbreeding, but:
    → Genetic drift eliminates diversity
    → One novel pathogen → everyone is genetically similar → all die
    → "500 people = 500 slightly different copies of the same immune system"
    
  → 5,000 people: long-term genetic viability threshold
    → But: 5,000 people cannot maintain antibiotics, semiconductors, or power grids
    → Genetic survival ≠ civilizational survival
    → You survive as a species. You survive as Stone Age hunter-gatherers.
`

## §7. The Stack of Required Conditions

`
For post-apocalyptic survival to be possible, ALL of the following must be true simultaneously:

  □ You have >3,700 people (minimum tech society)
  □ You have >5,000 people (genetic viability)
  □ You have >50,000 people (to maintain semiconductor-level tech)
  □ You have intact supply chains for food, water, energy, medicine
  □ You have institutional knowledge transmission (schools, universities)
  □ You have arable land not contaminated by radiation/toxins/salt
  □ You have accessible energy sources (not depleted fossil fuels)
  □ You have mineral deposits accessible with available technology
  □ You have a stable climate (not centuries of CO₂ disruption)
  □ You have no continuous external threats (raiders, diseases, climate extremes)

If ANY ONE of these fails → cascading failure → all die.

After full civilizational collapse:
  → ALL of these fail simultaneously.
  → Not "some survive, some don't."
  → "Everyone dies. The question is only: how long it takes."
