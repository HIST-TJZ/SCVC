## §2 Comparison of Three Strategies

`
Strategy A: Scientific attitude
  "I don''t know, but I will use more data to continue building the model."
  Cost: needs more information (bandwidth), more time (decision cycles)
  Benefit: model becomes more precise
  Applicable: when you have sufficient computational resources

Strategy B: Faith
  "I don''t know, I encapsulate this uncertainty with a ''supernatural agent.''"
  Cost: extremely low. Only needs one "cause" — "God did it."
  Benefit: anxiety decreases. Uncertainty is encapsulated.
  Applicable: when your computational resources are insufficient, but you need to survive.

Strategy C: Denial
  "This question doesn''t matter."
  Cost: lowest. But may miss real threats.
  Benefit: zero cognitive burden.

From an information-theoretic perspective:
  Faith = maximum compression model.
  Uses the fewest bits to explain the most phenomena.
  "Why is there a universe?" → 4 bits: "God created."
  "Why is there consciousness?" → 4 bits: "Soul."
  "Where do we go after death?" → 4 bits: "Heaven."
  
  4 bits vs science''s answer of 10⁶ bits.
  Compression ratio: 2.5×10⁵.
