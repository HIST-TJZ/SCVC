# SCVC Engineering Limit — E133: Maximum Tree Height

**SCVC Hard Inputs**: Water H-bond 0.20 eV (cohesion-tension), C-C bond 3.6 eV (wood strength), gravitational potential energy, stomatal conductance limits
**Related**: E4 (structural material ceiling), E65 (building height ceiling)

## §1 The Hydraulic Ceiling — Why Trees Can't Grow to the Sky

### 1.1 The Water Column Problem

```
Trees move water from roots to leaves via transpiration pull (cohesion-tension theory):

Physical constraints:
  → Maximum theoretical suction: ~-100 MPa (perfect vacuum = -0.1 MPa — but water under tension can go far beyond)
  → Water column tensile strength: ~-20 to -30 MPa (limited by cavitation)
  → Gravity: ΔP = ρgh → 0.01 MPa per meter of height
  → At 130m: hydrostatic pressure = 130 × 0.01 = 1.3 MPa
  → Cavitation risk rises linearly with height
  
  → "Every meter taller = 0.01 MPa more tension on the water column.
     At some height, the water column snaps (cavitates) → the tree dies from the top down."
```

### 1.2 SCVC Physical Chain

```
H-bond 0.20 eV → water cohesion:
  → Water molecules stick together via hydrogen bonds
  → Maximum tensile strength: ~20-30 MPa (theory), ~1-3 MPa (practical, due to nucleation sites)
  → At 130m: ~1.3 MPa → approaching the cavitation threshold in real xylem vessels

C-C bond 3.6 eV → wood strength:
  → Cellulose + lignin composite (wood) must support the tree's weight
  → Compression strength of wood: ~30-60 MPa (parallel to grain)
  → Weight of a 130m tree: trunk base stress ~10-15 MPa (from self-weight alone)
  → Wind loading adds ~5-10 MPa → total ~15-25 MPa
  → Safety factor: ~2-3× → marginal but sufficient

Photosynthetic limit:
  → CO₂ must diffuse through stomata → water lost in the process
  → At 130m: water transport resistance → stomata close → photosynthesis stops → no more growth
  → "Trees don't stop growing because they 'choose' to.
     They stop because at some height, they lose more water than they can replace."
```

## §2 The Redwood Ceiling

```
SCVC prediction: maximum tree height ~125-135m
Physical basis: water column cavitation at ~1.3 MPa gravitational tension

Reality:
  → Coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens): Hyperion — 115.9m (tallest known living)
  → Historical: ~120-130m estimated for some logged specimens
  → Australian mountain ash (Eucalyptus regnans): 114m (historical record ~132m)
  → Douglas fir: historical records ~120-126m

  → ALL converge near 120-130m
  → No tree species, on any continent, has reliably exceeded ~130m
  → "Not a coincidence. It's the H-bond ceiling on water transport."
```

### 2.1 Why No 200m Trees?

```
For a 200m tree:
  → Water column tension: 200 × 0.01 = 2.0 MPa
  → This exceeds the practical cavitation limit (~1.5-2.0 MPa in real xylem)
  → Even if water could reach the top, stomata would be permanently closed
  → No photosynthesis → no carbon gain → no growth

Possible escape routes (all physically forbidden):
  → Stronger water cohesion? ❌ H-bond 0.20 eV is fixed
  → Thinner xylem vessels? ❌ Flow rate ∝ r⁴ → too thin = no flow
  → Active pumping (like animals)? ❌ Energy cost prohibitive (would consume entire photosynthetic output)
  → "200m trees are not in our future. They are not in anyone's future.
     The H-bond will not be negotiated with."
```
