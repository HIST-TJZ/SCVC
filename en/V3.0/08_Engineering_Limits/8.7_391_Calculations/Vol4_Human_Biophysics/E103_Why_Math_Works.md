## §1 Wigner's Puzzle

### 1.1 "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences"

```
Eugene Wigner, 1960:

"The appropriateness of the language of mathematics for the formulation
 of the laws of physics is a miracle, a wonderful gift which we neither
 understand nor deserve."

Traditional view:
  Mathematics = abstract symbol system invented by humans
  Physics = objectively existing reality
  Why can human-invented abstract symbols describe objective reality?
  → This is "unreasonable"

Examples:
  π appears in circle circumferences (math), also in hydrogen atom energy levels (physics)
  Group theory (pure abstraction) → Standard Model of particle physics (describes reality)
  Riemannian geometry (pure abstraction) → General Relativity (describes gravity)
```

### 1.2 Three Historical Answers

```
(1) Mathematical Realism (Platonism):
    Mathematical objects "already exist" in an ideal realm
    Physics merely "instantiates" them
    Problem: Where is this ideal realm? How does "instantiation" work?

(2) Physicalism:
    Mathematics is merely a mapping of physical laws in the brain
    Math works because the brain is a physical object
    Problem: Why is the mapping so precise? Why does π appear in pions?

(3) Instrumentalism:
    Math is just a tool that happens to work well
    Problem: Why this "happens"? Probability is too low.
```
