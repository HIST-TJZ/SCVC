## Core Conclusion

$$\boxed{N_g = \text{Index}(D_{\text{Dirac}}) = 3}$$

Three fermion generations come from the Atiyah-Singer index theorem for the $\text{spin}^c$ Dirac operator on $CP^2$.

---

## Derivation

### $\text{spin}^c$ Structure

$CP^2$ is not a spin manifold ($w_2 \neq 0$) but admits a $\text{spin}^c$ structure. Physically, the $\text{spin}^c$ structure corresponds to the existence of a $U(1)$ flux (corresponding to hypercharge).

### Index Theorem

$$\text{Index}(D) = \int_{CP^2} \hat{A}(CP^2) \cdot \text{ch}(L^{1/2})$$

where:
- $\hat{A}(CP^2) = 1 + p_1/24 + \cdots = 1$
- $\text{ch}(L^{1/2}) = e^{c_1(L)/2}$, $L$ is the $\text{spin}^c$ line bundle
- $c_1(L)$ is determined by the BPS vortex winding number: $c_1 = 1$ (one zero mode per vortex)

### Zero-Mode Count

$$\text{Index}(D) = 3$$

Three zero modes correspond to three fermion generations. Each zero mode yields one complete fermion generation (SM representation $\mathbf{16}$) in the 4D effective theory.

## Parity Violation and Chirality

The complex structure $J$ of $CP^2$ distinguishes left- and right-handed fermions. The $\text{spin}^c$ Dirac operator index counts only chiral zero modes — automatically yielding a chiral gauge theory with parity violation. Left-handed fermions are $SU(2)_L$ doublets; right-handed are singlets.

$N_g = 3$ is a topological inevitability, not a coincidence of "why exactly three generations."
